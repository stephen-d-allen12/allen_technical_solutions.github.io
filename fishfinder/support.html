Common questions

How does it choose the spots?

It reads the shape of the lake and what's mapped on it: points, coves, creek mouths, bridges, docks, marinas, dams and islands. It works out the season pattern from the water temperature, then ranks each spot for your species, the season and the day's weather.

How accurate is it?

It's a forecast built from public data and well-known seasonal patterns, so treat it as your starting plan. Fish move with the weather, the bait and fishing pressure. If something looks wrong for your lake, let us know.

Which fish does it cover?

Bass and crappie. The bass plans are written for largemouth, with notes for smallmouth and spotted bass where they behave differently.

Which lakes can I search?

Lakes and reservoirs in the USA that have a name on OpenStreetMap, the free world map. Small or unnamed ponds may not show up.

Does it work without a signal?

Partly. Once you've opened a lake with a signal, the app, that lake and the parts of the map you looked at stay saved on your phone, so they still come up on the water. The forecast needs a signal to update.

Why does it turn red at night?

Red and amber light saves your night vision. The app switches at sunrise and sunset at the lake. Tap the sun or moon button to switch it yourself.

Does it use my location?

Only when you tap the location button, to show where you are on the map. Your location stays on your phone.

Where do the angler reports come from?

The app links to recent YouTube videos, Reddit posts, and state and guide fishing reports for your lake. They open on those sites.

Sonar and fish finders

Allen Fish Finder doesn't connect to sonar units yet, so there's nothing to pair. Pairing with fish finders is planned for the App Store and Google Play versions. Step-by-step pairing help will be on this page when it's ready.
